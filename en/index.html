02How I work · from question to decision

My way of doing things.

  1. 01

    I understand the business, not just the data

    Before I touch a line of code, I understand what decision is at stake. A model that answers the wrong question is expensive and elegant.

  2. 02

    I design the experiment or the model

    RCT, incrementality or Marketing Mix Model — whichever tool the question calls for. I measure the counterfactual: what would have happened without the campaign.

  3. 03

    I translate into the language of whoever decides

    From SQL to the boardroom, no translator in between, in Spanish or English. If it isn't understood, it isn't useful.

  4. 04

    I leave something that survives without me

    Reusable frameworks, automated pipelines, measurement your team can maintain. I don't sell dependency.

03What I believe · without false modesty

Create. Go deep. Transform.

What I love most is to create, and to get to the bottom of things. Everything that follows comes from there.

Marketing transforms, it doesn't just sell

Good marketing changes how someone sees the world, not just how much they buy. If all we move is the register, we've fallen short.

First, the human who sees the ad

Before the data and the model is the person who'll see the ad. What they feel, what moves them, what bores them: that comes first.

Comedy as a creative weapon

I work with comedians to make advertising no one else would. Humor isn't filler: it's how you say something true and make it stick.

Data that decides, not data that decorates

Work that doesn't change a decision is wasted work. We look deep and decide — we don't produce reports for the sake of it.

04Where it all comes from · the story

I never learned to be just one thing.

And it turns out that's the advantage. Three scenes explain how I work:

  1. The kid with the stereo. As a kid I took apart the home stereo to see where the music lived. I broke it and spent weeks fixing it. Break, understand, fix: that's what I do today with measurement.
  2. The economist who loved history. An economist from Los Andes, until I admitted the problem: the models assumed rational people, and people never read those books. I went to history and psychology to find them for real. That's why my models start from how people actually decide, not how they should.
  3. The one with the mic. I've done stand-up twice. They laugh or they don't: no dashboard dresses that up. That's how I present results — no anesthesia and no jargon.
